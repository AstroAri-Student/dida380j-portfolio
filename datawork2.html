Data Work 2: Trees of Vancouver

An investigation into trees bears interesting fruit

According to data provided by the City of Vancouver, there are a large number of trees planted throughout the many neighborhoods and boroughs of the city. But these trees are not evenly distributed. By comparing the relative size of neighborhoods, the number of trees planted in them, and the percentage of commuters who walk to work, we can uncover a few interesting trends.

Unsurprisingly, the number of trees in a neighborhood scales up with that neighborhood's land area. One might also assume that, given a larger number of trees beautifying the area, people would be more inclined to walk or cycle to work rather than take their cars. The data, however, does not seem to support this. The greatest indicator that a neighborhood would have many walking workers was its size.

Beautifying a neighborhood might contribute in some way to changing commuting patterns. But there is more to making a neighborhood walkable than just adding a few trees. For urban efforts to bear fruit, those efforts need to branch out.
